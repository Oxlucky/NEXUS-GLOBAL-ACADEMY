The NEXUS standard

Our guiding principle

We do not trade simply because the market is moving. We trade because our conditions have been met.

Capital preservation comes before capital growth. A trader must understand how to protect capital before focusing on growing it.
01 · Foundation

Purpose of this trading plan

This plan establishes the rules, procedures and standards students are expected to understand before trading with the NEXUS methodology. It is designed to reduce random trading, emotional decisions, unnecessary risk and inconsistent execution.

Every student should use this framework as a personal operating manual, review it regularly and record actual trading decisions in the NEXUS Trade Journal.

03 · Operating schedule

Trading hours

Official trading window: 8:00 AM–4:00 PM East Africa Time (EAT).
  • Prepare before the trading window begins.
  • Conduct normal analysis and execute new trades within the designated hours.
  • Do not open new trades outside the approved window.
  • Stop opening trades at 4:00 PM EAT and complete the end-of-day review.
  • Avoid unnecessary chart watching outside trading hours.

Cryptocurrency markets operate 24/7. The designated window is the academy's normal trading and execution schedule, not a claim that markets close outside it.

04 · Our approach

The NEXUS trading methodology

A. Market structure

  • Identify overall market direction and conditions.
  • Recognise bullish, bearish and consolidating markets.
  • Mark relevant support and resistance areas.
  • Recognise structural changes and important price levels.

B. Price action

  • Study candlestick behaviour, momentum and rejection.
  • Evaluate breakout strength and follow-through.
  • Distinguish genuine price movements from false breakouts.
  • Recognise exhaustion and avoid chasing extended moves.

C. NEXUS Impulse Entry Strategy

  • Identify balance and imbalance.
  • Recognise a genuine impulse rather than a weak or false breakout.
  • Wait for the strategy's required confirmation.
  • Define entry, invalidation and target before execution.
Indicators are not part of the official NEXUS methodology. Our framework is based on price action, market structure and confirmation.
05 · Before execution

Entry requirements

Before opening a position, students must establish:

  1. A clear directional bias.
  2. A relevant price level or trading area.
  3. A valid market-structure context.
  4. A genuine price-action opportunity.
  5. Confirmation according to the selected strategy.
  6. A logical stop-loss and invalidation point.
  7. A calculated position size.
  8. A planned take-profit and acceptable risk-to-reward.
No confirmation, no trade. If essential conditions are missing, reject the setup.
06 · Capital protection

Risk management standards

These are proposed educational limits for the NEXUS framework. They are not universally suitable for every student, account or instrument. Beginners should practise on demo first; live risk must be appropriate to personal circumstances and instrument volatility.

RuleProposed NEXUS limit
Risk per tradeUp to 0.5%
Maximum trades per day2
Maximum daily loss1%
Weekly loss review limit3%
Drawdown review threshold6%
Minimum planned risk-to-reward1:2
Protective stop-lossMandatory

Position sizing

Position size must be calculated from account equity, chosen risk, stop-loss distance and the instrument's contract specifications. Never choose a lot size simply because it looks profitable. Account for spreads, commissions, slippage and gaps where relevant.

A 1:2 planned risk-to-reward ratio is a planning threshold, not a guarantee that a target will be reached. Loss limits do not guarantee that losses cannot exceed them during fast markets or execution disruptions.

07 · Non-negotiable conduct

Daily trading rules

  1. Take no more than two trades per day.
  2. Stop trading after reaching the daily loss limit.
  3. Stop for the day after two consecutive losses.
  4. Never increase risk to recover a loss.
  5. Never enter a trade out of boredom or fear of missing out.
  6. Never widen a stop-loss to avoid accepting a planned loss.
  7. Never enter without strategy confirmation.
  8. Never trade under emotional pressure.
  9. Never use martingale or uncontrolled position doubling.
  10. Document every trade, including rule violations.
No trade is better than a bad trade. A day without a valid setup is a successful day of discipline.
08 · Open positions

Trade management

  • Respect the original trading thesis and protective stop-loss.
  • Do not interfere with a position purely because of fear.
  • Follow predefined take-profit and management rules.
  • Move to breakeven or trail a stop only when the strategy permits it.
  • Record management decisions and any deviation from the plan.
09 · Repeatable process

The NEXUS daily routine

Before trading
  • Review relevant economic news.
  • Identify market conditions.
  • Mark important levels.
  • Establish a bias and plan.
During trading
  • Wait for a valid setup.
  • Confirm conditions.
  • Calculate position size.
  • Execute with a stop-loss.
After trading
  • Stop new entries by 4 PM.
  • Record all trades.
  • Save chart screenshots.
  • Review mistakes and lessons.
Before the next session
  • Review journal patterns.
  • Identify one improvement.
  • Prepare without forcing a bias.
  • Return rested and focused.
10 · Execution control

The NEXUS entry checklist

Students should be able to answer YES to every essential requirement before entering a trade.

  • Am I within the approved trading hours?
  • Have I identified the market direction?
  • Is the trading area clearly defined?
  • Does market structure support my idea?
  • Is there valid price-action confirmation?
  • Is my entry technically justified?
  • Is my stop-loss correctly positioned?
  • Have I calculated my position size?
  • Is my planned risk within the limit?
  • Does the setup meet my planned risk-to-reward?
  • Am I emotionally prepared to accept a loss?
If an essential condition is not satisfied, do not enter.
11 · Measure what matters

Performance and accountability

Students evaluate decisions using evidence rather than emotion. Track:

  • Win rate and average win/loss.
  • Average realised risk-to-reward.
  • Net profit and loss.
  • Maximum drawdown.
  • Number of trades and frequency.
  • Strategy adherence and rule violations.
  • Performance by instrument and session.

A profitable result does not excuse poor execution. A correctly executed losing trade can still be a good trading decision.
